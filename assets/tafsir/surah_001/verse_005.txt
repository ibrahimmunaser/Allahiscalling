"It is You we worship and You we ask for help."

This verse is the heart of the surah and of Islam itself: worship belongs to Allah alone, and help is sought from Him alone. The order matters — worship is mentioned first as the purpose of creation, then seeking help, because the servant cannot fulfill worship without Allah's assistance. The shift from speaking about Allah to addressing Him directly ("You") marks the moment the recitation becomes a conversation. In the well-known hadith qudsi, Allah says this verse is "between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked for."
